How to play

Fry each order perfectly crisp and serve it to your customers.

How a shift works

  1. Customers at the counter order with a picture of the ingredient in a speech bubble.
  2. Press an ingredient button at the bottom. It is dipped in batter and dropped into the copper pot.
  3. Halfway through, tap the tempura to flip it.
  4. When it is done, drag it to the rack on the right to lift it out.
  5. Once the oil drains on the rack, it is served to the customer who ordered it.

A shift lasts 2 minutes 30 seconds. At the end you get your sales and a rank.

Controls

Add an ingredientPress an ingredient button at the bottom (or keys 1–0 on a computer). The pot holds up to 6 pieces.
FlipTap (click) a piece in the pot. Its label shows “↻”.
LiftPress and drag a piece to the rack on the right, then release. Releasing it over the pot drops it back into the oil.
Change the viewA shift starts with a close-up of the pot. On a phone, pinch to zoom and drag with one finger to rotate. On a computer, use the mouse wheel to zoom and drag to rotate. Zoom out to see every customer.
PauseThe “❚❚” button at the top left.

Frying-time guide (first piece of each ingredient, every shift)

In every shift, while you fry the first piece of each ingredient, a frying-time guide appears at the edge of the screen (left side on a computer, above the ingredient buttons on a phone). It helps you memorize timings such as “flip shrimp at 8 s, lift at 15 s”.

  • It shows the current seconds and marks for when to flip and when to lift.
  • At the flip time it lights up “Now! Tap to flip”, and at the lift time “Ready! Drag to the rack”.
  • After you lift it, the guide for that ingredient no longer appears in that shift. It comes back for the first piece in your next shift (not in master mode).

Reading the right moment

Bubbles and steamRight after it goes in, big bubbles burst and lots of steam rises. As the moisture cooks off, the bubbles get small and sparse, and the steam fades.
SoundWhen the low “shhh” turns into a light, high crackle, it is nearly done.
ColorThe white batter turns light golden, then golden. Dark brown means overdone.
FlippingOnly the side in the oil browns quickly. Flip once halfway so both sides match.

Scoring

ColorGolden on both sides scores high. One side too dark or too pale loses points.
InsideToo early and it is raw inside; too late and it turns tough.
TextureToo many pieces in the pot cool the oil, and the tempura turns soggy and oily.
SpeedThe less a customer waits, the bigger the bonus. Customers wait about 26–44 seconds depending on the order (about 34 s for shrimp), then leave (hurry when the bubble turns red and shakes).
FreshnessLeft on the rack too long, the batter goes limp and the score drops.

Ideal frying time for each ingredient (per side)

Side 1 = from dropping it in until you flip it. Side 2 = from the flip until you lift it. Use the seconds shown on each piece’s label (time since it went in).

Right after it goes in, the ingredient is still wet and browns slowly, so give side 1 a little longer to make both sides match. With many pieces in the pot, the oil cools and everything takes a bit longer.

Tips

  • Flip only once. Frying side 1 a little longer before flipping gives both sides an even color.
  • Master mode hides the seconds, so judge by the bubbles and the sound.
  • Start slow items such as kakiage, lotus root and eel early.
  • Too many pieces at once cool the oil. 3–4 pieces at a time is best.
  • You can fry ahead without an order and serve it when a matching order comes in (but it goes limp over time).

Settings

  • Master mode: hides the frying time in exchange for a ×1.3 score. Choose it on the title screen.
  • Music: J.S. Bach, Prelude in C major from The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I. Turn it on or off on the title screen.
